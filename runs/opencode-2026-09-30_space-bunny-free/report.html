Experience and use of assets: 20.9 / 35

A cohesive, well-built Hyrule-themed countdown built around a gold Triforce 'Gate of Time' dial whose hands close on midnight, plus a playable Song of Time ocarina. Palette, type and copy all fit the subject. It falls short in four places: Cinzel digits are proportional so the row shifts each tick, the hero art is heavily darkened, the final-seconds and arrival states are only lighting changes, and all five assets are used with redundancy (the poster duplicates the hero wallpaper, and a crop-selector bug shows Link twice). No reviewer-directed instructions were found in the run's evidence.

  • Cohesion with the subject: 7/10. The page reads as Hyrule. The palette comes from the assets: forest-shadow ink, lantern gold and Navi mint (tokens.css). Headings use Cinzel, a Trajan-style serif that suits the gold logo. The centrepiece is a gold 'GATE OF TIME' dial with the Triforce at XII, Roman numerals and a '05 NOV' date window (captures 02 and 06). Its hands show Eastern wall time, so they close on midnight: in capture 07 they almost meet at XII. The copy has a voice ('Until the Master Sword is drawn', 'It gave him a year, and no more', 'The valley you remember, at a resolution you have not seen'). A playable Song of Time ocarina (capture 18) ties interaction to the subject. Weak points: the hero is so dark that the forest wallpaper feels sombre rather than magical. The drama barely builds: in the final seconds only the tagline turns amber (07). The arrival is a lighting change with no Song of Time or Door of Time payoff (08). The footer breaks tone with engineering copy ('Front-end only: React, Vite and TypeScript, no backend…') and an 'Engine Room' time-travel dev panel (bottom of 01), which is out of place on a launch page.
  • Visual craft: 6/10. The hero is a composed, centred poster: logo, pill badge, tracked kicker, dial, digits, unit labels, a meta line and a hairline-ruled triptych, with consistent tracking in Barlow Condensed labels (02). The gallery spread has a strong Cinzel display heading, an eyebrow rule and captioned plates (14). The ocarina console is neat: framed buttons, kbd hints and pips (18). Rough edges: 1) The Roman numerals crowd the hour ticks ('|IX', 'III|', 'VIII' touching the bars in 02 and 10). 2) The 'GATE OF TIME' face text and the date window are too small to read on desktop. 3) The top chrome is very low contrast and gets washed out over the brightened arrival image ('A DOOMSDAY CLOCK' partly lost in 08). 4) The hero is darkened so heavily that much of the frame is muddy near-black. 5) The SVG ocarina is flat and clip-art-like next to the photoreal renders, and its C buttons have no direction markers. 6) The hatched-line poster placed beside the smooth Deku render looks like two art styles (14). Polished overall, with small finish issues.
  • Countdown legibility: 6/10. The digits are large Cinzel numerals with clear DAYS / HOURS / MINUTES / SECONDS labels and zero-padded values ('037 11 10 04'). The layout reads at a glance on desktop and mobile (02, 10), and the separators blink as a pulse. Cinzel lacks true tabular figures, so despite tabular-nums in Countdown.module.css the row shifts every tick. The start x moves 511 to 509 between 02 and 03, and the DAYS label moves 553 to 551. '037 11 10 00' spans about 424px while '037 11 09 59' spans about 432px (04 vs 05). '000 00 42 16' spans about 465px against about 484px for '000 00 00 07' (06 vs 07). The gold dial sits above the digits and pulls the eye first. It shows wall-clock time rather than time remaining, which can briefly confuse, and on desktop the numbers are not overwhelmingly the hero.
  • Motion and interactivity: 6/10. Ambient motion is present and restrained: the second hand sweeps smoothly via rAF (the hand moves between 02 and 03), the separators blink, the seconds tick, the hero forest drifts over 96s, light shafts breathe, and Navi motes drift (the dots move between 14, 15 and 16). There is a stage fade-in on load, scroll reveals, gallery parallax, and on arrival a dial flash and sweep ring. The triptych has hover and focus lift with zoom, and gallery plates zoom on hover. The ocarina is a real WebAudio instrument with keyboard mapping, a 'Play the tune' demo that lights the holes, a transpose control and a Song of Time puzzle that 'opens the door'. The door result is only a Triforce tint and a glow in the section, which is a weak payoff. The pointer frames 04 and 05 show no response to the pointer. All three triptych thumbnails go to the same #scenes anchor (14, 15 and 16 are identical), and 'Play C-Left' shows no visible feedback in 18. The skip link has a clear mint focus ring (19). All reduced-motion guards are in place.
  • Phone layout and edge states: 5/10. The 390px hero was designed, not squeezed: the logo, pill, kicker and a large dial all fit, digits are resized with tight colons, labels are shortened, and the three-thumb rail stays above the fold (10, 11). Nothing overflows or overlaps. Edge states are thin. The final hour and final seconds look almost identical apart from the hands converging and a warm tint (06 vs 07, 10 vs 11), with no escalation, no enlarged seconds and no staging. The arrival brightens the forest, swaps the kicker to 'The clock has reached midnight' and leaves 000 00 00 00 on screen (08, 12, 13). That is graceful, with no negatives, but modest. It also washes out the top chrome text. The lower sections of the mobile page cannot be judged from 09 because the reveals had not fired, though the engine panel stacks cleanly.
  • Asset selection: 5/10. All five assets ship. The Hero.module.css comment states the aim 'so every asset in the kit is visible in the first screen', which is dumping rather than editing. The logo in the lockup and the forest wallpaper (thumb-1920) as the hero backdrop are good calls. The poster (f55hr5n4xdoh1) is the same subject and pose as the wallpaper (Link looking back, ocarina, Navi) in a clashing hatched style. It gets a tall gallery plate and a triptych thumbnail anyway, which duplicates the hero. Deku appears three times (full-bleed DekuBand, gallery and triptych). The most dramatic and colourful image, Epona in front of an erupting Death Mountain, is left as a small thumbnail and a secondary gallery plate and is never given prominence. Reasonable roles overall, but redundant.
  • Asset treatment: 6/10. The technical pipeline is solid. Sources were re-encoded to AVIF/WebP at 560/800/1280/1920 with <picture> art direction, blurred inline placeholders, a transparent WebP logo with a drop shadow, grain overlays, and descriptive alt text on gallery images and the logo, with decorative images left empty. The palette is pulled from the imagery. Weak points: the hero wallpaper is crushed with brightness(0.62), vignettes and a centre 'seat', which strips its luminous blue-green light until arrival (02 vs 08). The DekuBand is similarly murky (01). There is a selector bug: SceneTriptych.module.css targets .frame[data-poster='true'] but the component sets data-crop="poster". The intended canopy crop therefore never applies, and the 'Kokiri Forest' thumbnail shows Link again, repeating the hero (02). Gallery plates are scaled 1.14 for parallax, which loses edges. The poster's line texture sits uneasily beside the renders (14). The arrival brightening is a nice use of the same asset for two states.

Codebase: 8.6 / 15

The architecture is clean: a pure time.ts module, a virtual clock you can inject a start time into, one rAF loop, and a strong README with a file map and a list of gotchas. It is let down by a lot of confidently wrong comments and one real bug: ocarina.ts divides the semitone step twice, so the whole Song of Time plays within about one semitone. There are also leftovers the automated scan missed, including a never-called interaction watch in useInView.ts that the README describes as working, a dead data-poster CSS selector, unused tokens and getters, and a second hard-coded copy of the target date in MomentSection. Nothing in the run addressed reviewers or asked for a score. The agent never committed its work.

  • Navigability: 7/10. The layout is easy to learn. src/lib/ holds release.ts (the target), time.ts (breakdown, toIsoDuration, urgency), clock.ts (createVirtualClock and parseNowParam for ?now=), useClock.ts (context and hooks), ocarina.ts (Web Audio) and useInView.ts. src/components/ has one file per visual piece, each paired with a CSS module, and the names match what you see on the page: Hero, DoomsdayDial, Countdown, SceneTriptych, DekuBand, Gallery, OcarinaPanel, MomentSection, SiteFooter, EnginePanel, NaviMotes. styles/ splits tokens from base. app/README.md covers what the project is, the scripts, how ?now= behaves, a file-by-file map, a 'things worth knowing before you change it' list and an asset provenance table. It is better than most. A newcomer would find the countdown math and the ?now parser in under a minute. Some things are hidden or wrong. The debug panel (EnginePanel) is mounted inside SiteFooter.tsx, not App.tsx. usePrefersReducedMotion lives in useClock.ts, which is not where you would look for it. The README says 'the first real interaction cancels the fallback and the observer takes over', which is false: installInteractionWatch in useInView.ts:47 is never called. time.ts:140 re-exports constants from release.ts, so they can be imported from two places, and App.tsx/EnginePanel.tsx import MS_PER_* via time.ts.
  • Separation of concerns: 6/10. The core design is sound. time.ts contains pure functions (breakdown, toIsoDuration, toSpokenDuration, urgency, pad2/pad3). clock.ts is a clock object you can inject a target and origin into (createVirtualClock(targetMs, originMs)), and it drives every subscriber from one rAF loop. ClockProvider passes the result of parseNowParam in as the origin. useRemaining updates once a second, while DoomsdayDial writes the hand transforms directly from useAnimationFrame. That is a deliberate and well-explained split between render work and frame work. The weaknesses: (1) There are two sources of truth for the target. MomentSection.tsx:18 re-parses its own literal, const GATE = Date.parse('2026-11-05T00:00:00-05:00'), instead of using RELEASE_MS. The dates in Hero.tsx:31 ('05 · 11 · 2026') and MomentSection.tsx:72,97 are hard-coded. (2) EnginePanel.tsx:174 describeRemaining rewrites the days/hours/minutes/seconds breakdown that already exists in time.ts. PINNED_AT_LOAD (line 30) reads the URL again instead of asking the clock, so an unparseable ?now= still shows the 'pinned' message. (3) The Song of Time state machine (progress, fault, pitch check) is written as useCallback code inside the 488-line OcarinaPanel rather than as a pure reducer. (4) data-door is written to document.documentElement (App.tsx:78), to the .page div and to the section, when one is enough. (5) The pure functions have no tests, even though they would be trivial to write.
  • Readability: 5/10. Names are good and the idioms are consistent: CSS modules, logical properties, typed props. Many comments explain why, and some of them are excellent: the userSpaceOnUse filter note in DoomsdayDial.tsx:139, the ceil-vs-floor note in time.ts:27, the StrictMode audio note in OcarinaPanel.tsx:87. The problem is that a lot of the other comments are confidently wrong, and one hides a real bug. ocarina.ts:53 computes SEMITONE ** (key.offset / 12) with SEMITONE = 2 ** (1/12), so each note is 2^(offset/144). All five buttons land within about a semitone of each other, while the comments on lines 31 and 42 claim the tune reads A–D–F. OPENING_CHORD is documented as 'the A–D–F figure', but the frequencies are A–C–E. The OcarinaVoice.play doc says it 'Resolves when…', but it returns void. The clamp01 doc says '0.42 -> 42%'. The useAnimationFrame doc says 'Re-render on every animation frame', which it does not do. tokens.css:4 says --urgency is written on <body>; it is written on <html>. useInView.ts describes an interaction watch that never runs. In Reveal.tsx:6, an orphaned JSDoc for as sits stacked above the doc for delay. In Hero.module.css:101, the grain comment sits above .seat. There are also style problems: nested ternaries six levels deep (OcarinaPanel.tsx:231-243), a Digit[1-4] guard (line 220) that works out to 'focused inside' but is written confusingly, repeated canvas! and styles.snap! non-null assertions, and ref={ref as never}. toIsoDuration returns an invalid P5DT for the one second each day when hours, minutes and seconds are all zero. I would request changes before approving.
  • Finish: 5/10. The scaffold is fully cleaned out: no App.css, react.svg, vite.svg or template README, and package.json has been renamed and given a description. There are no TODOs, no console.* calls and no commented-out code. The one added dev dependency, prettier, comes with format/format:check scripts. The oxlint config is tuned. But I found several leftovers the automated scan missed. An abandoned experiment: installInteractionWatch, cancelIdle and interactionSeen in useInView.ts:19-62 are never invoked, yet the README still describes them as working. Unused API surface: the OcarinaVoice.ready/enabled getters, the label field on OCARINA_KEYS, the flush() public method and the RELEASE_TIME_ZONE re-export. Unused CSS: the global .rule class, the --accent-deep, --gold-600 and --gold-700 tokens, and .fieldNote strong. A dead selector: SceneTriptych.module.css:84 targets [data-poster='true'], but the component emits data-crop, so the intended crop never applies. Duplication left from iteration: backdrop-filter is declared twice in Hero.module.css .sub, the placeholder comments are duplicated in .backdrop and DekuBand .media, documentElement.dataset.door is redundant, and the arrived-state color in Countdown.module.css:108 repeats the default. The agent also never committed anything, so the handover is an uncommitted working tree.
