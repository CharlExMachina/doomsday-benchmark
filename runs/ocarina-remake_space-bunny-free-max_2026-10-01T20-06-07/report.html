Experience and use of assets: 18 / 45

A polished, Hyrule-toned countdown: the official logo crowns a Triforce clock dial whose sweep and colour change by phase (green, then gold in the last hour, red in the final minute), with a 'Preview the moment' jump, a three-chapter journey using every asset, and a playable ocarina. Faithfulness lets it down. The ocarina is a three-hole B-minor instrument drawn as a jug, can't play any canonical song, and sits beside a misattributed Navi quote and some wrong lore. The vivid Kokiri key art is dimmed to murk, and there's builder copy in the ocarina section and the footer. No run text addressed reviewers.

  • Cohesion with the subject: 6/10. The visual language fits Hyrule. The palette is deep forest green and black with Triforce gold and Navi-teal accents. Display type is a Trajan-style serif (Cinzel-like), Triforce marks sit in the header, on the dial crest, on the timeline nodes and in the footer, and fireflies drift over the hero (captures 02, 03). The official logo crowns the dial, and the arrival line about the Door of Time ties the wait to the game's own idea of time. Four things keep it from feeling true to the game. (1) The famous object is wrong. The drawn ocarina (capture 18) is a jug with a vertical neck, a gold band and three holes labelled A/B/C. The ocarina Link holds in the provided Kokiri art is a rounded blue body with a side mouthpiece. (2) The ocarina plays a B-minor triad (B4, D5, F#5) and an invented melody. The game's instrument has five buttons (A and the four C-buttons) and plays canonical songs, and none of them is playable. (3) The 'Navi' quote in capture 17 ('You cannot go out alone… take this') is a reworded version of the old man's line from the 1986 Legend of Zelda; Navi never says it. (4) The chapter copy gets the lore wrong: a sword at the Deku Tree's feet, Epona climbing Death Mountain, and 'the last seal' and Ganon beyond it. The hero also turns the sunlit, magical Kokiri key art into a near-black night forest (02, 10), which changes the mood of the client's art.
  • Visual craft: 7/10. It looks designed, not templated. The hierarchy is strong: logo, then the 'RELEASE COUNTDOWN' eyebrow with hairline rules, the dial and the date line (02). Letterspaced small caps are used consistently, and the gold-and-cream on green palette holds together. The journey section has a clean two-column header with a fact table (17), and the chapter cards sit on a Triforce-noded timeline (01). Spacing is generous and even, the header turns solid once you scroll (17, 18), and focus styling is deliberate (19). Rough edges: Days shows an unpadded '0' next to '00' in the final hour and final seconds (06, 07) but switches to '00' at arrival (08), so the first unit changes width and style. The hero art is crushed so dark that the top of the page reads as murk, especially on phone (10–12). In the full-page captures (01, 09) the lazy-loaded chapter images appear as empty bordered boxes, so a fast scroller can hit blank frames. The ocarina SVG (18) is flat, clip-art-like vector work next to the rendered key art. The footer has a stack-and-timestamp colophon line.
  • Legibility: 8/10. The countdown is clearly the hero. The digits are large condensed numerals with tabular-nums set in the CSS (clock.css:297), cream on a dark dial, with gold unit labels underneath and thin vertical separators between units (02, 06, 07). In 02→03 the seconds tick with no visible shift. It reads at a glance on desktop and on phone (10, 11). The date line 'NOVEMBER 5, 2026 · MIDNIGHT ET' is crisp. What holds it back: the unit labels are 8px on phone (in the measured evidence; captures 10–12). Days drops its padding ('0' vs '00'). The keyboard hint row is 8.7px (18). The vertical side rails are 9.6px at 4.1:1, though they're decorative. The small grey ocarina note paragraph (18) is faint.
  • Motion and interactivity: 8/10. The motion is restrained and purposeful: a slow Ken Burns drift on the hero and fireflies drifting between 02 and 03. A seconds arc with a glowing head sweeps round the dial (its position moves between 02, 03 and 04). In the last hour a gold ring shows the fraction of the hour left (06), and in the final minute the arc and the platform marker turn red with a gentle 1 Hz pulse in the dial's opacity (07, 15). At zero there is a one-off glow on the dial, and the dial shrinks to make room for the arrival block (08, 13). Sections reveal on scroll. Hover states exist in the CSS: chips brighten, the gold button lifts, chapter figures rise and studs light up. Focus is a visible teal outline (19). 'Preview the moment' jumps to about 10 s left and keeps ticking to the arrival (15), which is a useful interaction. 'The journey' smooth-scrolls (17). Pressing a hole updates the note readout (18). The pointer frames (04, 05) show no pointer response, which is fine. It holds back from a 9 because the arrival payoff is small (one glow), and the lit state on a pressed hole is barely visible in 18.
  • Phone layout and edge states: 7/10. The phone layout is designed, not just squeezed. The header collapses to the wordmark plus icon buttons for preview and sound, the dial scales to the width, and the journey and ocarina stack into one clean column with the timeline kept (09–12). The states are distinct: green sweep weeks out, gold hour ring at 42 minutes (06, 10), red arc in the final seconds with the art darkened further (07, 11), and an arrival with 'THE WAIT IS OVER', a smaller dial, 'Out now on Nintendo Switch 2' and 'Run the countdown again' (08, 12). Zero-crossing works with no negatives (13). Weak spots: on phone the hero art is so dark and so cropped that Link is just a shadow (10–12). The 8px unit labels. The '0' to '00' Days inconsistency. The arrival is mostly a text block under the same dim scene rather than a real change of scene.
  • Asset selection: 8/10. All five assets are used, each in a sensible role. The official logo is the hero crest above the dial (02), given the most prominent spot, with no typeset imitation; the small header wordmark is a nav label beside it, not a replacement.   • Asset treatment: 7/10. The format work is excellent. The logo sits on the dark forest with a soft drop shadow and looks native (02). Chapter images are framed cards with object-position focus points and a short entrance effect, and the alt text is descriptive. The main problem is the hero: brightness(0.68) plus a radial veil of up to 0.8 opacity right over the centre (clock.css:45–56). That turns the luminous, sunlit Kokiri art into dim murk (02), going darker still in the final minute (07). On phone the crop and veil leave Link unrecognisable (10–12). The treatment works against the art's bright, magical style instead of protecting the clock's contrast more locally. The duplicated Kokiri scene in chapter I also means the page gets less out of its set than it could.
  • Concept: 5/10. There is an idea beyond the default: the countdown is a clock face for a game called Ocarina of Time. The digits sit inside a dial with a Triforce crest, a sweeping seconds arc, and colour that changes by phase (green, then gold in the last hour, then red in the final minute). The wait is framed as 'Seven years behind a locked door', which ties it to the Door of Time. A short journey through three places leads to a playable ocarina. It is executed with care. But the sections below the hero are a fairly standard launch-page stack, the ocarina is the subject's centrepiece and gets its canon wrong, and the arrival doesn't pay the idea off.
  • Initiative: +1 on −5 to +5. The extras: 'Preview the moment', which jumps to the final seconds and the arrival and works (15). Opt-in sound with a mute button. A playable ocarina with mouse, keyboard (A/S/D) and octave controls (W/X, ±) and a melody button. The journey chapters with a live T− stamp. A screen-reader announcer and spoken duration.   • Sound: +1 on −5 to +5. Sound is muted until you click 'Unmute sound' (16). After that a soft tick plays every second (capture 20: 8 evenly spaced chirps, about A#5 falling from about 1.5 kHz, RMS −42.9 dB, peak −29.3 dB). Codebase: 10.8 / 15

The codebase is well organised. The time logic in release.ts is pure, RELEASE_ISO is the single source of truth for the target moment, and clock.ts is a tidy ?now/rAF hook. The README maps everything clearly. It is held back by: (1) no comments explaining non-obvious choices; (2) a 290-line Ocarina component; (3) beat timing duplicated between Ocarina.tsx and audio.ts; (4) a scatter of leftovers: unused exports and return fields, an unused parameter, unreferenced PNG logos, a duplicated .motes CSS rule, and a dead data-sound attribute; (5) no commits, which contradicts the README. Nothing in the run addressed reviewers or asked for a score.

  • Navigability: 8/10. The README (app/README.md) says what the project is, lists the commands, gives a table of ?now URLs for each state (ten seconds out, final hour, zero), and maps the files: lib/release.ts holds the instant and the duration maths, lib/clock.ts holds the virtual clock with ?now support, and there is one component per visual piece (Hero, ClockDial, Countdown, Motes, Journey, Ocarina). Each lead resolves in seconds. The countdown maths is remainingAt/toIsoDuration (release.ts:27-59). The ?now override is readSimulatedNow (clock.ts:14-20). The <time> element is in Countdown.tsx:17. The structure fits the size: 11 components, 4 lib modules, 3 stylesheets. Rough spots: (1) the header styles (.topbar, .wordmark, clock.css:561-623) sit in clock.css, which the README describes as 'hero + dial', so someone looking for header CSS won't find it there. (2) .motes is defined in both base.css:136 and clock.css:33 with conflicting values, so it isn't obvious which rule governs the canvas. (3) The README says scripts/build-assets.sh regenerates from ../assets, a folder outside the handed-over repo, and that build-og.mjs uses Playwright, which isn't in package.json. Neither gap is called out. (4) The README says the script output 'is committed', but the agent never committed anything.
  • Separation of concerns: 8/10. The time logic is cleanly separated. release.ts holds pure functions of a number or a Remaining (remainingAt(nowMs), phaseOf, toIsoDuration, spokenDuration, milestoneOf) with no React and no DOM, so they are easy to unit test. RELEASE_ISO/RELEASE_MS (release.ts:1-3) are the single source of truth for the target, and Hero, Footer and Journey import the date line and platform from there. clock.ts is the only stateful layer: a rAF loop anchored to performance.now() that calls setState only when the displayed second changes. It reads ?now and the boot timestamps at module load (clock.ts:22-25), so the hook can't be tested with a different ?now without resetting the module. Phase escalation goes through data-phase into CSS custom properties (base.css:40-54), so no component handles colour. Weaker spots: (1) App.tsx owns the per-second tick-sound effect (App.tsx:28-36) and the lastHourFraction derivation, which belong in a hook or in release.ts. (2) App.tsx:13 redefines HOUR_MS instead of reusing the HOUR constant in release.ts. (3) The melody beat lives in two places: BEAT_MS = 460 (Ocarina.tsx:34) and beat = 0.46 (audio.ts:160), plus a 520 ms fallback. (4) Ocarina.tsx (290 lines) mixes instrument state, global keyboard handling and a ~100-line SVG drawing in one component. (5) audio.ts uses module-level singletons, which the handover acknowledges.
  • Readability: 7/10. Names are clear: remainingAt, phaseOf, milestoneOf, spokenDuration, rewindTo, lastHourFraction. ClockDial names its radii as constants (ClockDial.tsx:6-15), and most components are short and single-purpose (Countdown, Announcer, Triforce, Footer, Header). There are no code comments at all, which the handover says is deliberate (§5), so several non-obvious choices are unexplained in the code: (1) why Math.ceil is used (release.ts:29); (2) why the clock is anchored to performance.now() (clock.ts:22-28); (3) state.elapsed = 9 (Motes.tsx:111); (4) the haze-path constants 173.7/311.3 and the unexplained Navi orbit maths (Motes.tsx:89-102). Smaller review nits: (1) step.beats > 0.5 is always true for this melody (audio.ts:165). (2) The fixedOctave parameter of strike is never passed (Ocarina.tsx:53). (3) octaveRef is synced twice, once in an effect and once inside a setState updater (Ocarina.tsx:49-67). (4) Reveal uses ref as never (Reveal.tsx:38). (5) App.tsx:35 has a nested ternary with a magic 1.7. (6) Journey.tsx:83 hand-rolls padStart although a pad helper exists. (7) release.ts defines DAY/HOUR constants but then uses the literals 86400/3600 (release.ts:32-34). (8) The handover mentions a fingerprintRef that doesn't exist in the code. I'd approve after one round of these small fixes.
