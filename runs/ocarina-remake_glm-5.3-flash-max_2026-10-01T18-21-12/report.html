Experience and use of assets: 22.8 / 45

A strongly on-subject Ocarina of Time page. The official Kokiri Forest art darkens from day to a starry midnight as launch nears, and dawn breaks at zero; around it are gold-on-forest styling, Triforce ornaments, a clickable Navi, a calendar file and a playable five-hole ocarina with correct pitches. All five assets are used well and none is imitated. What holds it back: the raw ISO duration shows under the digits, the ocarina is drawn as flat clip art and its notes fail or click on very quick taps, phone shows moiré from the portrait art, and the footer has small builder and brief-framing lines. Nothing in the run addressed reviewers.

  • Cohesion with the subject: 8/10. Strongly Hyrule throughout: dark forest-green and gold palette pulled from the logo's gilt lettering, small-caps display serif echoing the logo, Triforce dividers and bullets, a floating Navi button with time-aware 'Hey! Listen!' lines (Navi.tsx), fireflies, and the Deku Tree quote 'Navi… Navi, where art thou? Come hither…', which is accurate to the game (01, 09). The day-to-night-to-dawn shift over the countdown (01 → 06/07 → 08) makes midnight feel like an event that belongs to the forest. Held back by the drawn ocarina (16–18): a pale cyan, flat, clip-art egg on a tube. It is lighter than the vivid cobalt Ocarina of Time Link holds in the key art and looks foreign beside the painterly artwork. The zero chime is a generic A-major arpeggio (audio.ts bells) rather than anything from the game.
  • Visual craft: 7/10. The hero is composed with care: centred logo, fixed-width gold digits with diamond separators, eyebrow and meta lines in spaced small caps, pill header chips (02). Story and ride cards have tasteful rounded corners, gold hairline borders and soft shadows (15). Rough edges: (1) the raw ISO duration string 'P37DT11H10M2S' sits as visible grey monospace text under the digits in every capture, an off-style element; (2) the 'Play the ocarina' heading is a small body-serif title while the other sections use large small-caps headings (01, 16), so the hierarchy is inconsistent; (3) the ocarina SVG is flat and clip-art-like next to the rendered art, and floats in a large empty dark band (16); (4) on phone, the portrait art's baked-in hatching/moiré shows across the hero (10–12); (5) the footer fine print is 0.62rem at 40% opacity.
  • Legibility: 8/10. Large gold serif digits in fixed-width reel cells (0.74em per cell in global.css), so ticks don't jitter. Diamond separators on desktop and a clear 2×2 grid on phone (10, 11); the remaining time reads at a glance in every state. Held back by the unit labels: 0.66rem with wide letter-spacing in dim ink over a busy photo (01 at 'DAYS/HOURS'). The visible ISO string adds noise right below the digits. On desktop the logo carries more visual weight than the countdown. The 9.3px A/S/D/F/G key hints on the pads are tiny.
  • Motion and interactivity: 7/10. Motion is purposeful. The digits roll like an odometer (0.45s cubic ease). The scene darkens toward night and brightens to dawn with the countdown (06/07 vs 01/08). Stars twinkle, fireflies drift, Navi floats and pulses, the hero has a scroll parallax, the scroll cue bobs, and the arrival title fades in from a blur. Ocarina pads press down with a gold glow and a rising ♪. A clear double gold focus ring shows on Tab (19). Held back by: the reel spins through every digit when it wraps (0→9, 59→00), which shows as half-cut digits mid-spin in 04; pads have no hover state, and Enter or Space on a focused pad does nothing (pointerdown only); the calendar button gives no confirmation after the click (15); the pointer frames (04/05) show nothing reacting to the pointer.
  • Phone layout and edge states: 8/10. The 390px layout is designed, not squeezed: portrait art swapped in for the hero, a 2×2 digit grid with separators hidden, the simulated-time chip and sound label hidden, cards stacked (09–12). Arrival is a designed moment: 'The clock has struck / The legend has returned / out now', a Triforce on the dial, the live pill reading 'The legend has returned', a dawn tint, and bells if sound is on. The zero crossing worked live (13). The final seconds are thinner: in the last minute only the digits brighten and the dawn veil starts, so 07/11 read almost the same as 06/10. The phone hero shows the source art's moiré, and the digits sit over Link's figure (09).
  • Asset selection: 9/10. All five assets are used in fitting roles: the official logo as the hero title, the wide Kokiri Forest art as the desktop hero, the portrait art as the phone hero (a smart pairing), the Deku Tree art for the 'Chapter one' story card, and Epona with Death Mountain for the 'Ride out' card. The strongest pieces (logo, forest art) get the most prominence. The one weak call is the portrait art: it carries a baked-in hatching pattern that shows on phone, so cropping the forest art may have served better there.
  • Asset treatment: 8/10. All images are converted to resized WebP with srcset and a separate phone source. The logo keeps its transparency and sits cleanly over the art. The hero art gets a time-driven veil and tint, so the official art itself turns to night and then dawn, a clever reuse of a single image (01 vs 06 vs 08). The cards use alt text that describes the scene; decorative hero images have empty alt, with an sr-only h1. Held back by: the moiré of the phone portrait art, which a darker or blurred treatment could hide (10–12); the art's own Navi glow sits right beside the logo shield on desktop (02, near 495,295) while a second, code-drawn Navi floats top-right; the 317 KB poster is heavy for an 800px image.
  • Concept: 8/10. There is a real organising idea beyond the hero-and-digits default: the Kokiri Forest moves from daylight through dusk into a starry midnight as launch approaches, a dial hand sweeps from 6 to 12 over the span, and at zero dawn breaks with a Triforce at the top of the dial (theme.ts stops, Dial.tsx; captures 01, 06, 07, 08). The playable ocarina extends the 'instrument of time' theme. It is executed visibly, though the dial is small and the ocarina section feels somewhat apart from the time narrative.
  • Initiative: +3 on −5 to +5. Useful extras that work: a downloadable .ics event at 23:45 ET (America/New_York TZID; the '15 minutes before launch' claim is correct); a playable five-hole ocarina with the canon pitches D4 F4 A4 B4 D5 and A/S/D/F/G hold-to-play keys; a clickable Navi whose line changes with the time left. All are on-subject and add to the page. Held back by: no song recognition (playing the Song of Time does nothing), the quick-tap audio bug, no confirmation after the calendar download, and no time shown in the visitor's own zone.
  • Sound: +1 on −5 to +5. Sound waits for the visitor: off by default, with a header toggle that has a clear on/off state and is remembered between visits; pressing an ocarina hole turns sound on, as the copy says. Pitches match canon (A4@2.94s, B4@3.58s, D5@4.21s detected), and the voice has a breath layer and delayed vibrato. But the recording (20) shows the D4 and F4 holes giving only broadband breath noise with no tone, while the A4/B4/D5 hits have bright full-band click lines at onset and a peak of −0.3 dB. noteOff reads env.gain.value straight after noteOn, so on very short taps the gain is either still near zero (no tone) or reads the default 1.0 (a loud click). The zero 'bells' are a generic A-major arpeggio rather than an Ocarina of Time motif. Net mildly positive, given that normal-length presses work.

Codebase: 10.9 / 15

A clean, conventionally organized React codebase: pure, testable time math in lib/time.ts, an isolated ?now virtual clock, one clock feeding a single Remaining value, and a README that maps every file. The main deductions are the dead audio.tick(), which README and HANDOVER both describe as a working feature, a hand-written duplicate of the target moment in the .ics generator, a misnamed activeRef/setActiveSafe pattern in OcarinaSection, unused CSS tokens, almost no why-comments, and no commits. Nothing in the run addressed reviewers or asked for a score.

  • Navigability: 8/10. The layout is conventional and right for the size: src/lib/ (time, theme, audio), src/hooks/ (useNow, useMotion), src/components/ (one file per visual piece) and src/styles/global.css. A newcomer finds the countdown math in lib/time.ts:18 (remainingFrom) and the ?now override in lib/time.ts:57-88 (getVirtualStart/currentTime), with hooks/useNow.ts driving it. Every visual piece has its own file (Countdown, Dial, Hero, Ambience, Navi, OcarinaSection, RideSection). README.md says what the project is, gives the run/build/lint commands, shows ?now examples and maps every important file, so finding things takes well under a couple of minutes. What holds it back: (1) global.css is one 1100-line file with no section comments. Component class prefixes (cd-, dial-, navi-, oc-, hero-) make it searchable, but you have to scroll or grep it. (2) hooks/useMotion.ts holds usePrefersReducedMotion, useParallax and useScrolled. None of them is called useMotion, and useScrolled has nothing to do with motion. (3) The README (README.md:37) lists "the per-second tick" as part of the audio engine, and that feature never plays (see Finish), so the map points at something that isn't there. (4) worldProgress, which drives the dial, is in theme.ts, while the anchor date it depends on is in time.ts.
  • Separation of concerns: 7/10. The time logic is well isolated. remainingFrom(nowMs), formatISODuration and remainingLabel in lib/time.ts are pure functions of their inputs and easy to unit-test. The virtual clock is cached at module level and kept to two functions. TARGET_ISO is the one constant the logic uses. State ownership is clean: one clock from useNow, then one Remaining built in App.tsx:17, which theme, dial, Navi and Countdown all derive from. The theme's pure sampler (sampleWorld) is separate from the hook that writes the CSS variables, but it isn't exported, so it can't be tested directly. What keeps the score down: (1) The target moment has a second, hand-written copy. The .ics in RideSection.tsx:11-12 hard-codes DTSTART;TZID=America/New_York:20261104T234500 and DTEND instead of deriving them from TARGET_MS, and DTSTAMP:20261001T120000Z is frozen at the day the agent worked. Hero (Hero.tsx:44,59) and StorySection also hard-code the date in copy. (2) useIsVirtualTime (useNow.ts:26) is a plain function with a hook's name. (3) The simulated-time formatting is done inline in App.tsx:38-45 when it belongs in Header. (4) The digit reel offset 1.16em is a magic number shared between Countdown.tsx:14 and the CSS (global.css:376,388,392) with no variable linking them. The parallax hook also writes scale(1.12) inline (useMotion.ts:28), which couples it to the CSS layout. (5) OcarinaSection puts audio wiring, keyboard handling, floating-note state and a 30-line inline SVG body in one 172-line component.
  • Readability: 7/10. Overall the code is tidy. Files are small, names are mostly clear (remainingFrom, naviMessage, DigitReel, noteOn/noteOff), JSX is idiomatic and there's no clever trickery. Problems I'd flag in review: (1) In OcarinaSection.tsx:33, const activeRef = active is named like a ref but is the state value, and setActiveSafe (line 34) isn't safer than anything. Because press depends on active, the window keydown/keyup listeners are torn down and rebound after every note. (2) theme.ts clamps the interpolation factor twice (in sampleWorld:77 and again in mixRGBA:29,35), which looks like debugging residue. mixRGBA builds a tuple with an awkward multi-line cast and then destructures it again. Helpers are declared out of order (worldProgress sits between mixRGBA and the toRGBAString it uses). There's a DAY constant, but hours are written inline as 30 * 60 * 60 * 1000. (3) In Countdown.tsx:27, days use String(...).padStart(2,'0') while the next lines use pad2, which does the same thing. The className is a nested ternary (line 32), and formatISODuration is computed twice per render (lines 68-69). (4) There are almost no why-comments where they're needed: the duplicate theme stop at 10 * 1000 that holds full night from 3h down to 10s, why performance.now is used for ?now, why the anchor is Sep 24, the 1.16em coupling, and the synth constants in audio.ts. The only comment in the source is /* private mode */. (5) aria-hidden="false" on the Navi wrapper (Navi.tsx:33) does nothing.
  • Finish: 7/10. There's no commented-out code, no TODOs, no stray debug logging (the one console.warn for an invalid ?now is intentional), no scaffold demo files, and build and lint are clean. Dependencies are justified: two @fontsource packages for self-hosted fonts, everything else from the scaffold. All six bundled images are referenced. Leftovers I found: (1) audio.tick() (lib/audio.ts:71-86) is dead. Nothing calls it, yet the README ("the per-second tick") and HANDOVER ("a soft per-second clock tick accompanies the visuals while sound is on") both describe it as a shipped feature. That is an abandoned, unwired feature with docs that claim otherwise. (2) The CSS tokens --bg-deep and --navi (global.css:3,9) are never used, and Countdown adds an is-zero class that has no styles. (3) .ics DTSTAMP is a frozen literal. (4) .stars i { top: 0 } is always overridden inline. (5) The agent never committed, so the handover is the scaffold commit plus one large uncommitted working tree with no history. (6) package.json still says "name": "app", version 0.0.0. The HANDOVER also describes the code a little inaccurately: it calls Ambience a "seeded" star field when it uses Math.random, and says "no timers besides the rAF loop" when Navi, the ocarina and audio all use setTimeout. Each of these is small, but together they read as not quite fully closed out.
